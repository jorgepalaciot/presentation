A Lean read of my aquaculture thesis

Draft — not yet published Case Studies · ~6 min read

In 2017 I wrote my industrial engineering thesis as a full business plan: a marine flounder (Paralichthys adspersus) aquaculture plant on Peru's central coast. Reading it back now with a Lean and process-improvement lens, some of it holds up — and some of it I'd approach differently today.

What the thesis actually was

The core question was whether it was technically and economically feasible to farm flounder for Lima's premium seafood restaurant segment, given that wild-catch supply covered only around 16% of demand. The plan located the plant in Chincha Alta, close to the target market, and worked through the full production cycle — broodstock, hatchery, nursery, grow-out, harvest, and primary processing — as one continuous operation.

The financial evaluation used a Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis (10,000 simulations) on the critical variables — mainly feed cost and sale price — which produced a positive NPV of S/. 6,728,598 and a 21% IRR, with a 96.87% probability of the NPV staying positive at a 95% confidence level.

Where process thinking already showed up

Even without calling it "Lean" at the time, parts of the thesis were already process work:

  • Process flow diagrams (DOP/DAP) for hatchery operations, live-feed production, and the harvest-to-packaging line — mapping each step before sizing equipment or staffing.
  • Facility sizing via the Guerchet method to calculate the actual area needed per workstation, instead of guessing at plant layout.
  • A production schedule built around tank utilization — staggering four parallel production batches specifically to avoid idle tanks between growth phases, which is a direct, if unlabeled, application of reducing waste (idle capacity) in a fixed-asset system.
