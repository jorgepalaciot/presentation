Flounder Aquaculture Plant — Business Case

Draft — Learning & Next step pending Business Plan Thesis · 2017

Undergraduate thesis project (B.Sc. Industrial Engineering, UCSM, 2017), structured as a full business case rather than a narrow feasibility note: a marine flounder (Paralichthys adspersus) aquaculture plant on Peru's central coast. This page walks through the case itself. For a separate, more personal look at what I'd change about the process thinking behind it today, see the companion blog article.

Problem

Wild-catch supply covered only around 16% of demand for flounder in Lima's premium seafood restaurant segment. The question the thesis had to answer: is it technically and economically feasible to close that gap with a dedicated aquaculture plant, rather than relying on extractive fishing?

Context

The thesis was scoped as a complete business plan, not just a feasibility memo. That meant a market study, plant design, an environmental impact assessment and a financial evaluation, all built around a single continuous production cycle — broodstock, hatchery, nursery, grow-out, harvest and primary processing — sited at Chincha Alta, close to the target market.

Approach

Three deliberate process-engineering choices shaped how the plan was built, not just how it was justified financially:

  • Process flow diagrams (DOP/DAP) for hatchery operations, live-feed production and the harvest-to-packaging line, mapped before any equipment or staffing was sized.
  • Facility sizing via the Guerchet method, to calculate the actual area needed per workstation instead of estimating the layout by eye.
  • Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis (10,000 simulations) on the two variables that actually drove the plan's risk — feed cost and sale price — instead of a single-point financial estimate.

Process design

The production schedule staggered four parallel production batches specifically to avoid idle tanks between growth phases — a direct, if unlabeled at the time, application of reducing idle capacity in a fixed-asset system.

Result

Under the modeled conditions, the plan produced a positive NPV of S/. 6,728,598 and a 21% IRR, with a 96.87% probability of the NPV staying positive at a 95% confidence level. The thesis concluded the plant was feasible on that basis. It's worth being direct about what this is and isn't: a thesis-level financial and operational case, evaluated and defended academically — not a plant that was ever built or operated.
